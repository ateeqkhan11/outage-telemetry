A reference clock for cloud outages

In development · pilot places open

A reference index in development, built on selected AWS us-east-1 API checks, with early data collection under way.

A parametric cloud-outage policy pays on two times: when the outage started and when it ended. The provider’s status page, crowd reports and outside measurement can give different answers to both, and the difference can decide whether a claim pays.

Three clocks for one outage

Each source answers “when did it start, and when did it end?” in its own way. None of them is wrong on its own terms; they measure different things.

ClockStarts whenEnds whenWhat shapes it
The provider’s status page The provider posts an incident. The provider marks it resolved. Posting follows the provider’s own checks, and an incident may be described as affecting only some customers or features.
Crowd reports Enough people notice a problem and report it. Reports fall away. What users see, including problems in their own networks, apps or other providers.
Outside measurement Calls made to the service from outside start failing, under a stated rule. The calls succeed again, under the same rule. Who makes the calls, from where, how often, and the exact rule that turns failed calls into an outage.

A policy has to name one clock and say exactly how it is read. Nimbus Index is building a reference clock from outside measurement, with the rules written down in full in the Methodology.

Key terms of the pilot

What the pilot is, as it stands today.

What it is
A reference record of when an AWS us-east-1 outage started and ended, for use in parametric cloud-outage cover.
Scope today
Selected API checks against AWS us-east-1: S3, DynamoDB, Lambda, EC2 and STS. Early data collection is under way.
Method
What counts as an outage, when the rules do not fire, and the scope with dates are set out in the Methodology.The rules are a draft, pending independent review.
Pilot
Free during the pilot, for a small group of firms, in exchange for feedback on what the data should cover and how outages should be defined.

Who it is for

Firms writing or backing parametric cover for cloud outages.

MGAs

Designing a cloud-outage cover and setting the terms a payout turns on.

Insurers

Writing parametric cloud-outage cover that needs an agreed clock.

Reinsurers

Judging the method behind a trigger before taking on the risk.
